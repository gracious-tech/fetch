# Everyday Version

The Everyday Version (EV) is a free, easy-to-read English Bible. It's based on the Readers' Version of the [Open English Translation](https://OpenEnglishTranslation.Bible/) (OET), but uses the familiar names of people and places that most readers already know.

<p><VPButton href='https://app.fetch.bible/#trans=eng_ev' text="Read the Everyday Version"></VPButton></p>


## Where did it come from?
The OET is a new translation from the original Hebrew and Greek by [Freely-Given.org](https://Freely-Given.org/). Its Readers' Version is written in clear, modern English.

Gracious Tech made the EV by taking the OET Readers' Version and making a small number of changes, which are listed below. Everything else, including the meaning and wording of the text, is the work of the OET's translators.


## Who is it for?
 * __Children__
 * __People still learning English__
 * __Anyone__ who enjoys reading a Bible that's easy to understand


## What makes it stand out?

### It's easy to understand
The EV keeps everything that makes the OET easy to read. It's written in everyday English and avoids church jargon, so readers don't need to learn a special vocabulary before they can understand what they're reading.

| | Mark 2:17 |
| --- | --- |
| __ESV__ | And when Jesus heard it, he said to them, “Those who are well have no need of a physician, but those who are sick. I came not to call the righteous, but sinners.” |
| __NIV__ | On hearing this, Jesus said to them, “It is not the healthy who need a doctor, but the sick. I have not come to call the righteous, but sinners.” |
| __EV__ | Jesus overheard them and said, “Healthy people don’t need a doctor—it’s the sick ones that do. I didn’t come here to help those who think they have no needs, but I came to call sinners.” |

### It uses familiar names
The OET spells names to sound more like the original Hebrew and Greek, such as _Yeshua_ for Jesus and _Avraham_ for Abraham, which is valuable for authenticity.

But many people already know these names in their traditional English form from church, school, books and films. The EV uses those familiar spellings for well-known people, places and books of the Bible, to avoid confusing those who may already be familiar with them.

Less well-known names keep the OET's spelling.

| | Matthew 1:2 |
| --- | --- |
| __OET__ | Avraham was the father of Yitshak (Isaac), Yitshak the father of Ya'akov (Jacob), Ya'akov the father of Yehudah (Judah) and his brothers. |
| __EV__ | Abraham was the father of Isaac, Isaac the father of Jacob, Jacob the father of Judah and his brothers. |

### It keeps God's name, Yahweh
Most English Bibles replace God's personal name with "the LORD". Like the OET, the EV keeps his name, Yahweh. God gave his people this name to remember him by:

> "Yahweh, the god of your ancestors, the god of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, sent me to you. This is my name forever, and this is my memorial throughout the generations."
>
> Exodus 3:15 (EV)

In a world with many religions and many ideas about "god", using his name also makes it clear who the one true God is.


## Other changes
Apart from the names, the EV makes only a few small technical changes. None of them change any translation decisions. The text itself is otherwise the same as the Open English Translation.
